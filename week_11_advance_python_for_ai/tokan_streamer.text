History_of_india = ''' History of India — Simple Overview
India has a very long and rich history, spanning thousands of years. It can be understood in these major periods:

1. 🏺 Ancient India
Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2500–1900 BCE) — Cities such as Harappa and Mohenjo-daro had planned streets, drainage systems, and advanced urban life.
Vedic Period (c. 1500–600 BCE) — Development of Vedic literature, social institutions, and early kingdoms.
Mahajanapadas — Several powerful kingdoms emerged.
Maurya Empire (c. 322–185 BCE) — Founded by Chandragupta Maurya; Ashoka later became one of its most famous rulers.
Gupta Empire (c. 4th–6th century CE) — Often associated with major developments in mathematics, astronomy, literature, art, and science.
2. 🏰 Medieval India
Various regional kingdoms and dynasties became powerful.
Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526) ruled large parts of northern India through several dynasties.
Vijayanagara Empire became a major power in southern India.
Mughal Empire (1526–1857) was founded by Babur. Famous rulers included Akbar, Jahangir, Shah Jahan, and Aurangzeb.
Shah Jahan commissioned the Taj Mahal.
3. 🇬🇧 British Rule
The East India Company gradually expanded its political control after the 18th century.
The Revolt of 1857 was a major uprising against Company rule.
In 1858, control of India passed directly to the British Crown.
The Indian independence movement grew under leaders and organizations including Mahatma Gandhi and the Indian National Congress.
4. 🕊️ Independence
India became independent from British rule on 15 August 1947.
Jawaharlal Nehru became independent India's first Prime Minister.
India became a republic on 26 January 1950, when the Constitution came into effect.
5. 🇮🇳 Modern India

Since independence, India has developed as a democratic republic with major growth in agriculture, industry, science, technology, education, and space research.

Easy timeline:
Indus Valley → Vedic Period → Mahajanapadas → Maurya → Gupta → Regional Kingdoms → Delhi Sultanate → Mughal Empire → British Rule → Independence (1947) → Republic (1950).'''
